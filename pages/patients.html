Morphea and scleroderma are not the same

These two terms get used interchangeably online. They are not the same condition, and telling them apart is one of the most useful first steps.

Stays in the skin

Morphea

A localised form of sclerosing skin disease affecting the skin and the tissue underneath, but not the internal organs. Subtypes vary widely, and knowing which one you have matters.

Read about linear morphea

Can involve organs

Scleroderma

A systemic condition that can involve the skin, blood vessels, and internal organs. Early recognition and specialist care are especially important.

Read about scleroderma

Going into an appointment prepared

Appointments can feel overwhelming. These three prompts help you get clarity and leave with a plan.

Before you go

Write down your symptoms, when they started, and what makes them better or worse. Bring a list of every medication you take, including supplements.

Questions to bring

What is my specific diagnosis? What should I watch for? Who should I call if something changes? Is there a support group you recommend?

See the full question guide

After your visit

Request a visit summary in writing and note the follow-up timeline. Write down any new terms you want to look up later — and bring this site as a starting point.
