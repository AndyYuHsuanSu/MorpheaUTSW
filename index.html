Understanding sclerosing skin disease

Morphea and scleroderma are often confused with each other, and rarely explained well. This site highlights what they are, how they differ, and what to ask your doctor — written by the dermatologist who treats them.

Two conditions, one distinction

These two terms get used interchangeably online. They are not the same condition, and the difference decides almost everything else about your care.

Stays in the skin

Morphea

A localised form of sclerosing skin disease. It affects the skin and the tissue beneath it, but not the internal organs. Subtypes vary widely, and knowing which one you have matters more than the label itself.

Read about linear morphea

Can involve organs

Scleroderma

A systemic condition that can affect skin, blood vessels, and internal organs. Early recognition and specialist care are especially important, which is why the distinction is worth getting right early.

Who writes this?
